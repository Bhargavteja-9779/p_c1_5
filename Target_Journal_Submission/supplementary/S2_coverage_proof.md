## S2. Coverage of the normalised jackknife+ window

**Setting.** Let $Z_i=(x_i,t_i)$, $i=1,\dots,n+1$, be exchangeable pairs of compound and retention time, where $Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ are the calibrants and $Z_{n+1}$ is the compound whose retention time $t_{n+1}$ is to be covered. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be any algorithm that maps a training multiset of such pairs to a prediction function $\mu$ and a scale function $\sigma>0$ and that is *symmetric*, i.e. invariant to the order of its training points. In the normalised jackknife+ window $\mu(x)=g(s(x))$, where $g$ is the monotone calibrant map and $s$ the fixed base-model score, and $\sigma(x)=\exp\{h(\phi(x,\mathcal{D},z_T))\}+10^{-3}$, where $h$ is fixed (trained on other chromatographic systems only) and $\phi$ depends on the training set $\mathcal{D}$ only through symmetric summaries (calibrant score range, gap to the nearest calibrant score, calibrant RT span, number of calibrants).

For $i\neq j$ let $(\mu_{-ij},\sigma_{-ij})$ be the output of $\mathcal{A}$ applied to all pairs except $Z_i$ and $Z_j$, and define the leave-two-out scaled residuals
$$R_{ij}=\frac{|t_i-\mu_{-ij}(x_i)|}{\sigma_{-ij}(x_i)}.$$
The quantities used by the method are computed from the calibrants alone: $\mu_{-i}=\mu_{-i,n+1}$, $\sigma_{-i}=\sigma_{-i,n+1}$ and $R_i=R_{i,n+1}$ for $i=1,\dots,n$. With
$$v^{\mathrm{hi}}_i=\mu_{-i}(x_{n+1})+\sigma_{-i}(x_{n+1})R_i,\qquad v^{\mathrm{lo}}_i=\mu_{-i}(x_{n+1})-\sigma_{-i}(x_{n+1})R_i,$$
the window is $C_\alpha(x_{n+1})=[v^{\mathrm{lo}}_{(k_{\mathrm{l}})},\ v^{\mathrm{hi}}_{(k_{\mathrm{u}})}]$, where $v_{(k)}$ denotes the $k$-th smallest of the $n$ values, $k_{\mathrm{u}}$ is $(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ rounded up and $k_{\mathrm{l}}$ is $\alpha(n+1)$ rounded down; the upper end is $+\infty$ if $k_{\mathrm{u}}>n$ and the lower end is $-\infty$ if $k_{\mathrm{l}}<1$.

**Proposition.** $P\{t_{n+1}\in C_\alpha(x_{n+1})\}\ge 1-2\alpha$.

**Proof.** *(i) Deterministic step.* Suppose that $t_{n+1}$ lies above the window. Then $k_{\mathrm{u}}\le n$ and $t_{n+1}>v^{\mathrm{hi}}_i$ for at least $k_{\mathrm{u}}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ indices $i$; for each of them $|t_{n+1}-\mu_{-i}(x_{n+1})|/\sigma_{-i}(x_{n+1})>R_i$. Because $\mu_{-i}$ and $\sigma_{-i}$ are computed from all pairs except $Z_i$ and $Z_{n+1}$, the left-hand side equals $R_{n+1,i}$, whereas $R_i=R_{i,n+1}$; hence $R_{n+1,i}>R_{i,n+1}$. If instead $t_{n+1}$ lies below the window, then $k_{\mathrm{l}}\ge 1$ and $t_{n+1}<v^{\mathrm{lo}}_i$ for at least $n+1-k_{\mathrm{l}}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ indices $i$, and again $R_{n+1,i}>R_{i,n+1}$ for each of them. Hence miscoverage implies that $\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{n+1,i}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ for the comparison matrix with entries $A_{ij}=\mathbb{1}\{R_{ij}>R_{ji}\}$ for $i\neq j$ and $A_{ii}=0$.

*(ii) Tournament step.* For any matrix of this kind (at most one of $A_{ij}$ and $A_{ji}$ equals 1), the set $S(A)$ of indices $j$ with $\sum_{i\neq j}A_{ji}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ has at most $2\alpha(n+1)$ elements (Barber et al., Ann. Stat. 49 (2021) 486–507, proof of Theorem 1). Because $\mathcal{A}$ is symmetric and the $Z_i$ are exchangeable, the distribution of $A$ is invariant under simultaneous permutations of its rows and columns, so all indices have the same probability of belonging to $S(A)$, and $P\{n+1\in S(A)\}=E|S(A)|/(n+1)\le 2\alpha$. Combining (i) and (ii) gives the claim. ∎

**Remarks.** (1) The argument uses no property of $\sigma$ other than positivity and symmetry, so any difficulty model trained independently of the target data may be used; this is why the difficulty model is learned exclusively on other chromatographic systems and its calibrant-dependent inputs are recomputed for every leave-one-out set. (2) Using the scale computed from the full calibrant set for the calibrants' own residuals (the "no leave-one-out refit" ablation) breaks the symmetry and is not covered by the proposition; empirically, its mean coverage at $n=20$ and $\alpha=0.10$ was {{abl.P_naive_noLOO.coverage_mean|.3f}} (Table S17). (3) The guarantee is marginal over the random draw of calibrants and compound from the compounds analysed on the target system; it does not hold when calibrants are selected non-randomly (e.g. a fixed standard mixture). Such selections were therefore evaluated empirically (robustness experiments and NORMAN data), and the selection-aware calibration of Section 2.6 of the article addresses the case of a known selection rule. (4) The window is finite if and only if $k_{\mathrm{u}}\le n$, i.e. $n\ge 1/\alpha-1$. (5) A Monte-Carlo check with a known heteroscedastic scale, $n=20$ and $\alpha=0.10$ is part of the unit tests (`code/tests/test_core.py::test_normalised_jackknife_plus_coverage_exchangeable`).
