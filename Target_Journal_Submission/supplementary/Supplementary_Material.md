## S1. Dataset details

Retention times were taken from RepoRT v1.0 (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.16267768). For each chromatographic system, entries without a valid
retention time, standardised SMILES or standard InChIKey were removed. Compounds were identified by the first 14 characters (connectivity block)
of the standard InChIKey; entries of the same connectivity block (stereoisomers and duplicate entries) were averaged if their retention times
differed by at most 0.2 min and removed otherwise. The mobile-phase pH class was derived from the reported eluent additives (acidic if any acid
additive was present in eluent A or B, basic if any basic additive and no acid, buffered if only ammonium salts, none otherwise) and the organic
modifier from the reported solvent fractions (acetonitrile, methanol, mixed or other). Column length, particle size, temperature, flow rate and
dead time were taken from the metadata and the gradient duration from the gradient table. Missing numeric metadata were encoded as 0 (temperature, flow rate and dead time were missing for 116, 33 and 37 of the 297 RP systems). For the NORMAN methods, which do not report these three descriptors, they were passed as missing values (NaN); because the gradient-boosted difficulty model never saw NaN for these features during training, LightGBM routes NaN like 0 (verified with lightgbm 4.7.0: identical predictions for NaN and 0), so that both data sets are encoded identically. Reversed-phase systems
with at least 60 compounds after cleaning were target systems, except METLIN SMRT, which served only as training data; HILIC systems with at
least 60 compounds formed the out-of-mechanism test set. Fig. S1 summarises the target systems and Table S1 the numbers of training systems
available in each leave-source-out fold.

## S2. Coverage of the normalised jackknife+ window

**Setting.** Let $Z_i=(x_i,t_i)$, $i=1,\dots,n+1$, be exchangeable pairs of compound and retention time, where $Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ are the calibrants and $Z_{n+1}$ is the compound whose retention time $t_{n+1}$ is to be covered. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be any algorithm that maps a training multiset of such pairs to a prediction function $\mu$ and a scale function $\sigma>0$ and that is *symmetric*, i.e. invariant to the order of its training points. In the normalised jackknife+ window $\mu(x)=g(s(x))$, where $g$ is the monotone calibrant map and $s$ the fixed base-model score, and $\sigma(x)=\exp\{h(\phi(x,\mathcal{D},z_T))\}+10^{-3}$, where $h$ is fixed (trained on other chromatographic systems only) and $\phi$ depends on the training set $\mathcal{D}$ only through symmetric summaries (calibrant score range, gap to the nearest calibrant score, calibrant RT span, number of calibrants).

For $i\neq j$ let $(\mu_{-ij},\sigma_{-ij})$ be the output of $\mathcal{A}$ applied to all pairs except $Z_i$ and $Z_j$, and define the leave-two-out scaled residuals
$$R_{ij}=\frac{|t_i-\mu_{-ij}(x_i)|}{\sigma_{-ij}(x_i)}.$$
The quantities used by the method are computed from the calibrants alone: $\mu_{-i}=\mu_{-i,n+1}$, $\sigma_{-i}=\sigma_{-i,n+1}$ and $R_i=R_{i,n+1}$ for $i=1,\dots,n$. With
$$v^{\mathrm{hi}}_i=\mu_{-i}(x_{n+1})+\sigma_{-i}(x_{n+1})R_i,\qquad v^{\mathrm{lo}}_i=\mu_{-i}(x_{n+1})-\sigma_{-i}(x_{n+1})R_i,$$
the window is $C_\alpha(x_{n+1})=[v^{\mathrm{lo}}_{(k_{\mathrm{l}})},\ v^{\mathrm{hi}}_{(k_{\mathrm{u}})}]$, where $v_{(k)}$ denotes the $k$-th smallest of the $n$ values, $k_{\mathrm{u}}$ is $(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ rounded up and $k_{\mathrm{l}}$ is $\alpha(n+1)$ rounded down; the upper end is $+\infty$ if $k_{\mathrm{u}}>n$ and the lower end is $-\infty$ if $k_{\mathrm{l}}<1$.

**Proposition.** $P\{t_{n+1}\in C_\alpha(x_{n+1})\}\ge 1-2\alpha$.

**Proof.** *(i) Deterministic step.* Suppose that $t_{n+1}$ lies above the window. Then $k_{\mathrm{u}}\le n$ and $t_{n+1}>v^{\mathrm{hi}}_i$ for at least $k_{\mathrm{u}}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ indices $i$; for each of them $|t_{n+1}-\mu_{-i}(x_{n+1})|/\sigma_{-i}(x_{n+1})>R_i$. Because $\mu_{-i}$ and $\sigma_{-i}$ are computed from all pairs except $Z_i$ and $Z_{n+1}$, the left-hand side equals $R_{n+1,i}$, whereas $R_i=R_{i,n+1}$; hence $R_{n+1,i}>R_{i,n+1}$. If instead $t_{n+1}$ lies below the window, then $k_{\mathrm{l}}\ge 1$ and $t_{n+1}<v^{\mathrm{lo}}_i$ for at least $n+1-k_{\mathrm{l}}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ indices $i$, and again $R_{n+1,i}>R_{i,n+1}$ for each of them. Hence miscoverage implies that $\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{n+1,i}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ for the comparison matrix with entries $A_{ij}=\mathbb{1}\{R_{ij}>R_{ji}\}$ for $i\neq j$ and $A_{ii}=0$.

*(ii) Tournament step.* For any matrix of this kind (at most one of $A_{ij}$ and $A_{ji}$ equals 1), the set $S(A)$ of indices $j$ with $\sum_{i\neq j}A_{ji}\ge(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ has at most $2\alpha(n+1)$ elements (Barber et al., Ann. Stat. 49 (2021) 486–507, proof of Theorem 1). Because $\mathcal{A}$ is symmetric and the $Z_i$ are exchangeable, the distribution of $A$ is invariant under simultaneous permutations of its rows and columns, so all indices have the same probability of belonging to $S(A)$, and $P\{n+1\in S(A)\}=E|S(A)|/(n+1)\le 2\alpha$. Combining (i) and (ii) gives the claim. ∎

**Remarks.** (1) The argument uses no property of $\sigma$ other than positivity and symmetry, so any difficulty model trained independently of the target data may be used; this is why the difficulty model is learned exclusively on other chromatographic systems and its calibrant-dependent inputs are recomputed for every leave-one-out set. (2) Using the scale computed from the full calibrant set for the calibrants' own residuals (the "no leave-one-out refit" ablation) breaks the symmetry and is not covered by the proposition; empirically, its mean coverage at $n=20$ and $\alpha=0.10$ was 0.708 (Table S17). (3) The guarantee is marginal over the random draw of calibrants and compound from the compounds analysed on the target system; it does not hold when calibrants are selected non-randomly (e.g. a fixed standard mixture). Such selections were therefore evaluated empirically (robustness experiments and NORMAN data), and the selection-aware calibration of Section 2.6 of the article addresses the case of a known selection rule. (4) The window is finite if and only if $k_{\mathrm{u}}\le n$, i.e. $n\ge 1/\alpha-1$. (5) A Monte-Carlo check with a known heteroscedastic scale, $n=20$ and $\alpha=0.10$ is part of the unit tests (`code/tests/test_core.py::test_normalised_jackknife_plus_coverage_exchangeable`).

# S7. Laboratory-level guarantee of cross-system calibration (XSC)

**Setting.** A *laboratory* is a pair (T, D): a chromatographic system T with its population of analytes (the compounds whose
structures are to be filtered on T) and a draw D of n calibrants by the laboratory's selection rule ρ. The laboratory fits the
monotone map ĝ and the scale τ̂ (Section 2.6) on D; for a multiplier c its windows are
C_c(x) = ĝ(s(x)) ± c·τ̂·σ(x), and its *own retention* is

  cov_T(c) = P_{x ~ analytes of T}{ t(x) ∈ C_c(x) | D } ,

a random quantity (it depends on T and D). In the evaluation, cov_T is the empirical retention on the analytes measured on T (all compounds other than the calibrants), and V_k below is computed in the same way; the guarantee therefore concerns the empirical retention on the measured analytes. Because C_c(x) grows monotonically in c, the smallest multiplier that gives the
laboratory a retention of at least 1 − ε,

  V_T(ε) = inf{ c : cov_T(c) ≥ 1 − ε } ,

is well defined, and cov_T(c) ≥ 1 − ε ⇔ V_T(ε) ≤ c.

**Assumption (system exchangeability).** The target laboratory (T, D) and S training laboratories (s_1, D_1), …, (s_S, D_S)
are exchangeable, where D_k is drawn on s_k by the same rule ρ and the same n, and the base score and the difficulty model used
on s_k did not use data from s_k (cross-fitting by source group; main text, Section 3.3). No assumption is made on the distribution of
compounds within a system, on the shape of the error distribution or on the quality of the base model.

**Proposition 1 (exact level, one draw per system).** Let V_k = V_{s_k}(ε) computed on the held-out compounds of s_k and
ĉ = the ⌈(1 − δ)(S + 1)⌉-th smallest of V_1, …, V_S (ĉ = ∞ if this index exceeds S). Then

  P{ cov_T(ĉ) ≥ 1 − ε } ≥ 1 − δ.

*Proof.* V_T, V_1, …, V_S are exchangeable, so the rank of V_T among the S + 1 values is uniform (ties broken at random), and
P{V_T ≤ ĉ} ≥ ⌈(1 − δ)(S + 1)⌉/(S + 1) ≥ 1 − δ — the standard split-conformal argument [Vovk et al. 2005; Lei et al. 2018]
applied to laboratories instead of compounds. The claim follows from cov_T(ĉ) ≥ 1 − ε ⇔ V_T ≤ ĉ. ∎

The probability in Proposition 1 is over the laboratory (T, D) *and* the S public systems. The guarantee is therefore of PAC type
with respect to the laboratory's own calibrant draw: it bounds the probability that the retention achieved with the actual
calibrants is inadequate, not merely the average retention, which is all that jackknife+ or split conformal windows provide with
n ≈ 20 calibrants (main text, Section 4.1). It is marginal with respect to the public systems. A guarantee conditional on the
public set as well would require a binomial correction of the order index (Vovk 2012; Park et al. 2022). With S ≈ 200 it would
raise the index by a few ranks, and we did not apply it. It needs no
minimum n: n enters only through the replayed protocol. For n ≥ 1/α − 1 this is the cross-system analogue of PAC/
training-conditional conformal prediction [Vovk 2012; Park et al. 2022, meta-learning PAC prediction sets].

**Draw-averaged version (used in the main text).** With several replay draws per system (R = 8 here), we use the system-level
CDF F_k(c) = (1/R)Σ_r 1{V_{k,r} ≤ c} and choose the smallest c with (1/S)Σ_k F_k(c) ≥ (1 − δ)(1 + 1/S). This reduces the
Monte-Carlo noise of a single draw per system, but its finite-sample validity is only approximate. For averages of
within-group CDFs the worst-case level is 1 − 2δ (Dunn et al. 2023). The exact rule is therefore evaluated alongside it. For 90 % of the
protocols the two multipliers differ by less than 10 %, and their failure rates on held-out systems are reported in
the main text (Section 4.2) and Fig. S3. The grid of multipliers ends at c = 40: an entry ∞ in Table S28 means that the required
multiplier exceeds 40, so that the windows are effectively unbounded (this occurs only for σ ≡ 1 with the 'most polar' rule).

**Average-retention version.** Choosing the smallest c with (1/S)Σ_k cov_{s_k}(c) ≥ 1 − α controls the expected retention
over laboratories, E cov_T(c) ≥ 1 − α, up to an O(1/S) term (selection-aware calibration of Section S6 is the special case
in which the window shape is that of jackknife+).

**What the guarantee does not cover.** (i) Laboratories whose system or analyte population is systematically unlike the
public systems (e.g. a new column chemistry or a mobile-phase pH absent from RepoRT) violate exchangeability; leave-source-out
evaluation and the external NORMAN methods test this empirically (main text, Sections 4.2 and 4.6). (ii) The analytes of a laboratory are
represented by the measured compounds of public systems; analytes that are systematically harder than these (e.g. outside the
applicability domain of the base model) are covered only to the extent that the difficulty model σ widens their windows.
