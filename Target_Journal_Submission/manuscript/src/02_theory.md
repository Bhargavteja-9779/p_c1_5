# Theory and calculation

## Problem formulation

Let $T$ denote the chromatographic system used in a non-target screening (NTS) study and $t(x)$ the retention time (RT) of compound $x$ on $T$. A laboratory measures a set of $n$ calibrants $\mathcal{D}_n=\{(x_i,t_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}$ on $T$ and has access to public RT data acquired on other systems. For a candidate structure $x$ proposed for an LC-HRMS feature with observed retention time $t_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the candidate is retained if $t_{\mathrm{obs}}\in C_\alpha(x)$, where $C_\alpha(x)$ is an RT window. The quantity that matters for annotation is the probability that the *correct* structure survives the filter, $\mathbb{P}\{t(x)\in C_\alpha(x)\}$ (coverage); its complement is the false-rejection rate of the filter. The second quantity is the fraction of incorrect candidates removed, which decreases as windows widen. We therefore seek windows whose coverage is controlled at a user-chosen level $1-\alpha$ for compounds of the target system, using only the $n$ calibrants and data from other systems, and that are as narrow as this constraint permits.

## Base retention-order score

Absolute RTs change strongly between systems, whereas elution order is far better conserved [@kretschmer2026order]. We therefore learn a system-independent score $s(x)$ whose ordering agrees with the elution order observed within each training system. Training data are all RP systems from sources other than that of $T$ (plus a 15,000-compound subsample of the METLIN SMRT system [@domingo2019smrt]). A gradient-boosted ranking model (XGBoost, pairwise objective with query groups = chromatographic systems [@chen2016xgboost]) is fitted on 217 RDKit two-dimensional descriptors and a 1024-bit Morgan count fingerprint (radius 2) [@rdkit; @rogers2010extended]. Because only the order of $s$ is used, no per-system normalisation of RTs is required.

## Monotone calibrant mapping

On the target system the score is mapped to time by a monotone function $g$ fitted on the calibrants: isotonic regression of $t_i$ on $s_i$, followed by piecewise-cubic Hermite (PCHIP) interpolation through the isotonic fit [@fritsch1980monotone], and linear extrapolation beyond the calibrant score range with the Theil–Sen slope of the calibrants. Monotonicity encodes the assumption that the base score preserves elution order on $T$; deviations from this assumption (selectivity changes) appear as residuals and are absorbed by the window. Leave-one-out (LOO) maps $g_{-i}$ are obtained by refitting without calibrant $i$.

## Conformal retention-time windows

*Jackknife+.* With LOO residuals $R_i=|t_i-g_{-i}(s_i)|$, define for a compound $x$ the $n$ values $v^{\mathrm{hi}}_i(x)=g_{-i}(s(x))+R_i$ and $v^{\mathrm{lo}}_i(x)=g_{-i}(s(x))-R_i$. The jackknife+ window [@barber2021jackknife] is
$$C_\alpha(x)=\left[\,v^{\mathrm{lo}}_{(k_{\mathrm{l}})}(x),\ v^{\mathrm{hi}}_{(k_{\mathrm{u}})}(x)\,\right],$$
where $v_{(k)}$ denotes the $k$-th smallest of the $n$ values, $k_{\mathrm{u}}$ is $(1-\alpha)(n+1)$ rounded up and $k_{\mathrm{l}}$ is $\alpha(n+1)$ rounded down. If calibrants and the compound of interest are exchangeable draws from the compounds analysed on $T$, then $P\{t(x)\in C_\alpha(x)\}\ge 1-2\alpha$ for any mapping algorithm that treats the calibrants symmetrically, as the monotone map does, and coverage is close to $1-\alpha$ when the fitted map is stable [@barber2021jackknife]. As for conformal prediction in general [@angelopoulos2023gentle], the window is finite only if $k_{\mathrm{u}}\le n$, i.e.
$$n\ \ge\ \frac{1}{\alpha}-1,$$
so that at least 9 calibrants are needed for $\alpha=0.10$, 19 for $\alpha=0.05$ and 4 for $\alpha=0.20$.

*Normalised jackknife+ with a meta-learned difficulty model (proposed).* RT errors after transfer are heteroscedastic: they depend on the compound (e.g. ionisable groups whose charge state differs between mobile phases, compounds dissimilar to the training data [@matyushin2024uncertainty]) and on where the compound falls relative to the calibrants (interpolation versus extrapolation [@malm2025projection]). Normalising the nonconformity score by an estimate of the local difficulty is a standard way to obtain adaptive conformal intervals [@papadopoulos2011regression; @lei2018distribution]. Let $\sigma(x;\mathcal{D})>0$ be a difficulty (scale) function. With $\sigma_{-i}$ denoting the difficulty function computed with calibrant $i$ left out of $\mathcal{D}_n$ and normalised scores $R_i=|t_i-g_{-i}(s_i)|/\sigma_{-i}(x_i)$, the window is defined as above with
$$v^{\mathrm{hi}}_i(x)=g_{-i}(s(x))+\sigma_{-i}(x)R_i,\qquad v^{\mathrm{lo}}_i(x)=g_{-i}(s(x))-\sigma_{-i}(x)R_i.$$
Because $\sigma_{-i}$ is computed from the leave-one-out calibrant set by the same symmetric procedure that produces $g_{-i}$, the argument of Barber et al. [@barber2021jackknife, Thm. 1] applies verbatim to the scaled score, and the $1-2\alpha$ guarantee is retained (proof in Supplementary Section S2). The function $\sigma$ itself is learned only from other chromatographic systems and is therefore fixed with respect to the target data. Learning the scale or nonconformity function on related tasks has precedents in few-shot and meta-learned conformal prediction [@fisch2021fewshot; @park2024fewshot]; here, the related tasks are other chromatographic systems, and the scale function is conditioned on the calibrant geometry and on routinely reported system metadata.

## Meta-learned difficulty model

The difficulty model is $\sigma(x;\mathcal{D})=\exp\{h(\phi(x,\mathcal{D},z_T))\}+10^{-3}$ min, where $h$ is a gradient-boosted regression tree ensemble (LightGBM [@ke2017lightgbm; @lightgbm]) and $\phi$ concatenates five feature groups: (i) *physicochemical* descriptors (logP, TPSA, molecular weight, H-bond donors/acceptors, rotatable bonds, fraction sp³, ring counts, molar refractivity, Labute ASA, element counts); (ii) *ionisation* features (SMARTS counts of carboxylic, sulfonic and phosphonic acids, phenols, primary/secondary/tertiary aliphatic amines, quaternary nitrogen, amidines/guanidines, pyridine- and imidazole-type nitrogens, formal charge); (iii) *novelty* features (maximum Tanimoto similarity of $x$ to the base-model training compounds and an exact-match flag); (iv) *calibrant-geometry* features computed from $\mathcal{D}$ (position of $s(x)$ relative to the calibrant score range, extrapolation distance, gap to the nearest calibrant score, calibrant RT span, number of calibrants); and (v) *system* descriptors $z_T$ from routinely reported metadata (mobile-phase pH class, organic modifier, USP column class, column length, particle size, temperature, flow rate, gradient duration, dead time). $h$ is trained to predict $\log(|r|+0.01\ \mathrm{min})$ for transfer residuals $r$ generated by *simulating the calibrant protocol* on training systems: for each training system, random calibrant sets of size 10, 20 and 40 are drawn (three draws each), the monotone map is fitted, and residuals of all remaining compounds are recorded. To make these residuals representative of transfer to an unseen system, base scores for each training source group are produced by a base model trained without that group (inner cross-fitting, Fig. 1).

## Selection-aware system-level calibration

The guarantee above requires calibrants that are exchangeable with the analytes, which a fixed calibrant mix need not be. If the rule $\rho$ by which a laboratory selects its calibrants (for example, "the most commonly measured compounds" or "the chemicals of a given standard mix") is known, it can be replayed on $S$ training systems, which are then the exchangeable units, as in conformal inference for hierarchical data [@dunn2023distribution; @lee2026hierarchical]. With $\mathrm{cov}_s(c)$ the coverage on training system $s$ of windows in which every scaled score $\sigma_{-i}(x)R_i$ is multiplied by $c$, the selection-aware factor is
$$c_\rho=\min\left\{c:\ \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{cov}_s(c)\ \ge\ 1-\alpha\right\},$$
computed with base and difficulty models that did not see system $s$; the target system's windows are then built with $c_\rho$. The resulting control is of average coverage over systems that share the selection rule and the analyte population, not a per-system finite-sample guarantee.

## Comparator windows

All comparators use the same base score, the same calibrants and (where applicable) the same monotone map: (B1) Gaussian window $g(s(x))\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{LOO}}$, representing the common practice of setting the tolerance from calibration residuals; (B2) penalised cubic B-spline (P-spline [@eilers1996flexible]) mapping with the classical prediction interval $\hat t\pm t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-\mathrm{edf}}\,\hat\sigma\sqrt{1+h(x)}$ and smoothing parameter chosen by generalised cross-validation, analogous to the projection intervals of PredRet [@stanstrup2015predret]; (B3) Gaussian-process projection (RBF + linear + white-noise kernel) with Gaussian predictive intervals, with hyper-parameters fitted by maximising the marginal likelihood of the target calibrants — unlike the GP projection of García et al. [@garcia2022probabilistic], whose prior is meta-learned on other chromatographic methods; (B4) split conformal prediction [@papadopoulos2002inductive; @lei2018distribution] with half of the calibrants used for fitting and half for calibration; (B5) unnormalised jackknife+; (B6) jackknife+ normalised by the spread of five bootstrap-replicate base models (a standard heuristic uncertainty signal [@matyushin2024uncertainty]), computed leave-one-out consistently; the nearest-neighbour-similarity term foreseen for B6 in the analysis plan was not implemented; and (B7, post hoc) a GP projection whose hyper-parameters are meta-learned on the training systems, our implementation of the meta-learned-prior idea of García et al. [@garcia2022probabilistic]: a constant + linear + RBF kernel on the calibrant-standardised score and RT, with the five hyper-parameters chosen per fold to maximise the predictive log-likelihood of held-out compounds after simulated calibrant draws on the training systems (inner cross-fitting as for the difficulty model) and then applied without any tuning to the target systems.
