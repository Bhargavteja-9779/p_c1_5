# Conclusions

Retention-time windows derived from parametric error models — the Gaussian ±z·RMSE rule, smoothing-spline and Gaussian-process projection intervals — do not keep their nominal retention rate when an RT model is transferred to a new chromatographic system with a few calibrants; on {{n_target_systems}} systems with 20 calibrants, Gaussian-process intervals discarded the correct structure {{gp_miss_factor|.1f}} times as often as intended. Calibrant-based jackknife+ windows restore the promised rate without any training data from the target system, and a difficulty model meta-learned across systems makes them narrower and more reliable for compounds outside the calibrant range and for late eluters, especially in long gradients. Two practical rules follow: at least $1/\alpha-1$ calibrants are needed (19 for 95 % windows), and the window is only as trustworthy as the representativeness of the calibrants. Conformal windows do not remove more false candidates than conventional windows at the same retention, but they make the retention rate a design choice rather than an unknown — which is what a filter that silently discards the right answer requires. Future work should calibrate windows jointly with MS/MS and collision-cross-section evidence and address structurally novel compounds, for which marginal coverage does not imply conditional coverage.
