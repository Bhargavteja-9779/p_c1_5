# Conclusions

Conformal RT windows built from a laboratory's calibrants keep their nominal retention only on average. With 20 calibrants, about a third of laboratories retained fewer than 90 % of the correct structures, and conventional windows did worse. Cross-system conformal calibration replaces this marginal promise with one that concerns the laboratory itself. The laboratory's calibrants place and scale the window, and the public record of hundreds of chromatographic systems calibrates its width for exactly the laboratory's protocol. Retention of at least 90 % was then achieved in at least 90 % of laboratories ({{x.random.20.XSC_sigs_lab.fail|pct}} failing), on systems from source groups never seen in training and in 37 external NTS laboratories. With their own calibration mixes, the variant without the meta-learned scale kept the guarantee. The method gives finite, guaranteed windows from three calibrants and for calibrant mixes that do not resemble the analytes. A meta-learned difficulty model, which added little within calibrant-only conformal windows, became useful as a prior that stabilises a laboratory's error scale and narrows guaranteed windows on systems resembling the public data. It did not transfer to external methods with incomplete metadata, where σ ≡ 1 is the safe default. The real-world case study also puts current practice in perspective. In the NORMAN laboratories, heuristic RT filters removed more isomers only by discarding the correct structure for one suspect in seven to ten, and with the guarantee respected their calibration mix supported little filtering, although RT remained useful for ranking. Because the multiplier for any calibrant design can be computed from public data before measurement, the approach also tells a laboratory which calibrants are worth measuring. The guarantee requires the laboratory and its analytes to resemble public systems and their compounds. Growing repositories will extend its reach, and the same cross-system construction applies to collision cross sections and other system-dependent evidence. Combining such laboratory-level guarantees across RT, collision cross section and MS/MS evidence is the natural next step towards identification with stated error rates.
