# Conclusions

Retention-time windows derived from parametric error models, fixed tolerances or residual rules do not keep their nominal retention rate when an RT model is transferred to a new chromatographic system with a few calibrants: with 20 calibrants, Gaussian-process intervals discarded the correct structure {{gp_miss_factor|.1f}} times as often as intended and fixed ±1 min windows kept only {{fw.FIX_1min.n20.coverage_mean|pct0}} of the correct structures. Calibrant-based jackknife+ windows restored the promised rate on {{n_target_systems}} reversed-phase systems, on HILIC systems and on {{norman.n_systems}} external interlaboratory methods, without any training data from the target system, provided that at least $1/\alpha-1$ calibrants are measured. A difficulty model meta-learned across systems made the windows narrower and more reliable for compounds outside the calibrant range in long reversed-phase gradients, but it was not a universal improvement. The decisive practical condition is that the calibrants resemble the analytes: calibrant mixes of common or unrelated compounds broke the guarantee, a known selection rule could be corrected by replaying it on public data, and a difference between the calibrant and analyte populations could not. Conformal windows do not remove more false candidates than conventional windows at the same retention; their value is that the retention rate becomes a design choice rather than an unknown — which is what a filter that may silently discard the right answer requires. Future work should calibrate RT windows jointly with MS/MS and collision-cross-section evidence and develop calibrant designs and conditional guarantees for structurally novel compounds.
