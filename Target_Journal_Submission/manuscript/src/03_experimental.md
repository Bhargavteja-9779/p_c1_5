# Experimental

## Data

RT data for model development and the main evaluation were taken from RepoRT v1.0 [@kretschmer2024report; @report2025zenodo], a curated repository of small-molecule RTs with standardised structures and chromatographic metadata (CC BY-SA 4.0; archive SHA-256 4eeeb46a…6091); the external validation used the NORMAN interlaboratory data [@malm2025projection] (Section 4.10). The target systems stem from the BGC data set of Harrieder and Witting deposited in RepoRT, the studies of Folberth et al. [@folberth2020ms2], Souihi et al. [@souihi2022multiconditionrt] and Stoffel et al. [@stoffel2022alkylpyridinium], the PredRet collection [@stanstrup2015predret] and further sources (Table 1). Compounds were identified by the connectivity block of the standard InChIKey; stereoisomer entries within a system were averaged when their RTs differed by ≤0.2 min and removed otherwise. Mobile-phase pH class and organic modifier were derived from the reported additives and solvents. Target systems were all reversed-phase (RP) systems with at least 60 compounds after cleaning, excluding METLIN SMRT, which served only as an auxiliary training library: {{n_target_systems}} systems with {{n_rt_values_targets}} retention times (median {{median_compounds}} compounds per system; Table 1, Fig. S1). HILIC systems with ≥60 compounds were analysed separately as an out-of-mechanism test ({{n_hilic_systems}} systems).

## Evaluation protocol and leakage control

Systems were partitioned by data source into five leave-source-out folds ({{fold_list}}). For each fold, the base model, the bootstrap ensemble (B6) and the difficulty model were trained exclusively on systems from the other four folds (and SMRT; numbers of training systems per fold in Table S1), so that no retention time or chromatographic system of a held-out source group entered model development, as required for a realistic estimate of performance on new systems [@aca2023validation] (compounds, by contrast, are shared across sources; see below). The only retention data taken from a target system were those of its calibrants; the difficulty model additionally used the system's reported chromatographic metadata. Within each target system, calibrant sets of size $n\in\{10,15,20,30,40\}$ were drawn uniformly at random (20 draws per $n$, fixed seeds), and all remaining compounds of the system formed the test set; windows were computed for $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ (P-spline, GP and ensemble-normalised windows only for $n\in\{10,20,40\}$, as pre-specified, because of their computational cost). The primary setting ($n=20$, $\alpha=0.10$), the comparators, metrics and statistical tests were specified in an analysis plan committed to version control before the full run (code/EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md); a preceding smoke test with small models on two systems per fold revealed one implementation error in comparator B6, which was corrected before the full run (plan addendum A). The plan was not registered with an external registry; the only deviation from it is the omitted similarity term of B6 (Section 2.7). Because many compounds of a target system also occur in public data from other laboratories ({{share_seen_test}} of test compounds at $n=20$ were present in the base model's training data), test compounds were additionally stratified into those seen and not seen by the base model, and by their maximum Tanimoto similarity to its training set.

Pre-specified robustness experiments used $n=20$ and 10 draws per system with (i) calibrants spread over the RT range (one random compound per RT quantile stratum), (ii) the 20 compounds of each system most frequently measured across RepoRT, (iii) Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 2 % of the RT span added to the calibrant RTs and (iv) one calibrant shifted by 10 % of the span. Generalisation was assessed with a second base model (a LightGBM regressor trained on SMRT only) and on HILIC systems (base and difficulty models trained on HILIC systems from other sources), and sensitivity to the difficulty-model hyper-parameters by varying the number of leaves (15 or 63, with 400 trees) or of trees (200 or 800, with 31 leaves), with 10 draws per system. Analyses added after the plan was written but before the corresponding results were computed are labelled post hoc: fixed-tolerance windows on the same calibrant draws, very small calibrant sets ($n=5$ and 8), the external validation on NORMAN data, the selection-aware calibration and the meta-learned GP comparator B7 (plan addenda B–E; B7 was evaluated on the identical calibrant draws, verified by recomputing the jackknife+ coverage of the first draw at $n=20$).

## Cross-system calibration, candidate lists and external laboratories

XSC (Section 2.6; plan addendum G, committed before the run) was evaluated in the same leave-source-out folds. For each fold, the laboratory's protocol was replayed on every training system with at least 40 compounds ({{x.S_min}}–{{x.S_max}} systems per fold; 8 replays per system and protocol). The replays used base scores from a model trained without the fold and without the system's own source group, and a difficulty model trained without that source group, so that no system calibrated itself. Protocols were $n\in\{3,5,10,20,40\}$ calibrants chosen at random, spread over the RT range, as the compounds measured most often in public data (counted over public systems outside the laboratory's source group), or as the most polar compounds (lowest computed logP; a deliberately unrepresentative rule). The multipliers were then applied unchanged to the held-out target systems, with the calibrant draws of the main experiment (20 draws per system for random calibrants, 10 otherwise). The laboratory-level target was $\varepsilon=0.10$, $\delta=0.10$, fixed before the run.

*Real candidate lists.* For each test compound, the candidate list was every structure in PubChemLite for Exposomics (version 2026-09-25, {{xc.n_pcl}} structures [@schymanski2021pubchemlite; @pubchemlite2026zenodo]) with the same molecular formula and a different InChIKey connectivity block, as an accurate-mass or formula query would return. Lists were capped at 200 randomly chosen isomers per formula (median list size {{xc.median_list}}). A candidate was eliminated when the observed RT of the feature fell outside the candidate's window. We recorded the retention of the correct structure, the fraction of candidates eliminated, the number retained and the rank of the correct structure among the candidates ordered by the standardised RT deviation $|t_{\mathrm{obs}}-g(s(x))|/\mathrm{scale}(x)$ ($n=20$ random calibrants, draws 0–4).

*External laboratories.* For the 37 reversed-phase NTS methods of the NORMAN interlaboratory comparison [@malm2025projection], multipliers were calibrated on all RepoRT systems (each scored by a model that excluded its own source group) and applied without change. Two protocols were used: $n\in\{5,10,20\}$ random calibrants, and each laboratory's calibration chemicals as calibrants with the suspects as analytes. For the second protocol, the replayed rule was "the NORMAN calibration chemicals measured on the system" ({{xn.S_mix}} RepoRT systems contained at least ten of them).

## Metrics

For each system and draw (a 'laboratory') we computed the retention, i.e. the empirical coverage (fraction of test compounds whose RT lies in their window); the primary endpoint for laboratory-level control is the *failure rate*, the share of laboratories whose retention falls below $1-\varepsilon=0.90$ (target $\le\delta=0.10$). We also computed mean and median window width (min), and the Winkler interval score [@winkler1972decision; @gneiting2007strictly]
$$\mathrm{IS}_\alpha(l,u;t)=(u-l)+\frac{2}{\alpha}(l-t)\,\mathbb{1}\{t<l\}+\frac{2}{\alpha}(t-u)\,\mathbb{1}\{t>u\},$$
a proper score that rewards narrow windows and penalises misses. Because it does not by itself prevent an under-covering method from scoring well, widths are compared only between methods that meet the same guarantee. Candidate-filtering performance was evaluated with *isomeric decoys*: for each test compound, up to 30 RepoRT compounds with the same molecular formula but a different connectivity block were treated as incorrect candidates; a decoy was eliminated when the observed RT of the true compound fell outside the decoy's window (draws 0–4 at $n=20$).

## Statistical analysis

Failure rates are reported with 95 % confidence intervals from a cluster bootstrap over target systems (draws of one system resampled together; 2,000 resamples) and per held-out source group, because the 228 systems come from five source groups whose results are not independent. The unit of analysis is the target system; metrics were averaged over draws within each system. Mean coverage is reported with 95 % bootstrap confidence intervals over systems (10,000 resamples for the main analyses and 1,000–4,000 for the supplementary analyses) and, as a sensitivity analysis, with a cluster bootstrap over source folds. Coverage validity was assessed with the pre-specified one-sided bootstrap test of the null hypothesis that mean coverage is at least $1-\alpha$ (10,000 resamples of systems under the null; cluster bootstrap over source groups as sensitivity analysis). The proposed method was compared with each comparator by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests [@wilcoxon1945individual] on per-system interval scores, mean widths and absolute coverage gaps $|1-\alpha-\text{coverage}|$, with Holm correction within each metric family [@holm1979simple] and the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation as effect size [@kerby2014simple]. Robustness experiments used 10 draws per system.

## Implementation

All computations ran on a 4-core CPU without GPU, with Python 3.13, RDKit 2026.09.2 [@rdkit], XGBoost 3.4.1 [@chen2016xgboost], LightGBM 4.7.0 [@ke2017lightgbm], scikit-learn 1.9.1 [@pedregosa2011scikit] and SciPy 1.18.1 [@virtanen2020scipy]. The analysis plan and its addenda were time-stamped in version control before the corresponding runs; every number, table and figure was generated by scripts from the raw outputs, without manual transcription. The code is available from the corresponding author on request.
